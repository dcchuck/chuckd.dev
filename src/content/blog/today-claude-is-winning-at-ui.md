# Today, Claude Is Winning at UI

*November 2026*

## To the Point

Claude has been impressive at turning the UI I’m picturing into reality. A brief prompt and the existing application give it enough context to fill in details I haven’t explicitly described. That’s the part that stands out to me: how much of what I intended comes through without my having to spell it out.

I find Codex takes more back-and-forth. A new page may not fit the application the way I expect, and getting it there takes several attempts to explain what feels off.

That’s what “better” means here: closer to what I expected, with less effort from me. It’s a personal judgment about the work I’m doing in November 2026. Yours may differ. Mine will change.

## With Humor

I grew up using Windows. When Windows 7 came along, I changed its appearance to look like the older Windows I remembered fondly. Microsoft had spent years improving the interface. I went looking for the setting to put it back.

Now I have access to tools that can build an interface from a description. Naturally, I’ve used them to put the old Windows on my personal website.

Some people ask for a pelican riding a bicycle. I want my gray rectangles back.

These little personal tests have their uses. Benchmaxxing aside, creating a good benchmark is difficult. The work in front of you gives you another way to form an opinion, even if it won’t produce a particularly useful leaderboard.

I’ve added a few themes to this site to reflect my interests and tastes. For this one, I asked Codex for Windows 7 Classic, with the old layout. I wanted it to feel familiar to someone who had actually used it.

I had both tools build fresh implementations, using context gathered from screenshots. Codex made progress, but even after several rounds of feedback, the details were off.

You can [try the Windows Classic theme right here on this site](/?theme=windows-classic). Move the windows around, open Paint, or lose a few minutes to Minesweeper.

To me, Codex’s version looks like a knockoff of Windows. Claude’s is much closer to what I remember. If you’re looking at these thinking, “Chuck, those both look like Windows,” I suspect you didn’t spend much time using the old one. Look at the folders, the toolbars, the little details.

But that’s the point about personal expectations. I have a particular Windows in mind, and Claude got closer to it without my having to explain every detail. Both started fresh by gathering screenshots for context.

There’s still something for me to examine in what I gave Codex. “Please reproduce my childhood, but pay more attention to the toolbars” may leave some room for interpretation.
